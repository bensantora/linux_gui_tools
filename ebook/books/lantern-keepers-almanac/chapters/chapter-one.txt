Chapter One: The Long Dark

In midwinter the village lay under a blanket of snow so thick that the church bell sounded muffled, as if it were ringing from inside a pillow. The lamplighter, a stooped man named Osric, walked the lanes at four o’clock each afternoon with his long brass pole.

He never hurried. Each lamp received the same patient attention: a lift of the glass, a careful touch of flame, a gentle lowering. By the time he reached the bridge, the whole street glowed like a string of pearls.

## The Arithmetic of Dusk

Osric kept a ledger of how many minutes of daylight each week gave and took away. In December the numbers shrank. In January they hesitated. By February they began, very slowly, to grow.

“Light does not return all at once,” he liked to say. “It comes back a spoonful at a time.”

## Try This

Note the time of sunset each day for one week. Write it down without comment. Notice how the small differences add up.
