Preface

I wrote this book on winter evenings, with a cold cup of tea at my elbow and a window that rattled whenever the wind came down from the hills. It began as a notebook of observations about light: the pale light of morning, the amber light of a kitchen at dusk, the thin light of a distant harbor.

The notebook grew. Friends asked to read it, then neighbors, then strangers who had heard about it from neighbors. Eventually it became a book, and you are holding it, or at least looking at it on a screen, which is close enough.

You do not need to read it in order. Open it anywhere, as you would open a window. If something here makes you look up from the page and notice the light in your own room, then it has done its job.

My thanks go to the baker on Alder Street, who let me sit by her oven, and to the old ferryman, who told me the best stories and charged me nothing for them.

— J.Q.S.
