Chapter Two: The Thaw

Spring arrived in the valley the way a shy guest arrives at a party: first a rumor, then a glimpse near the door, and finally, all at once, everywhere. The creek woke up and began to talk. The orchard dripped. The roofs steamed in the morning sun.

Margit, who kept bees on the south slope, went out each day to check the hives. She wore a gray scarf and carried a small wooden stool, because she believed that any task worth doing was worth doing while sitting down.

## What the Bees Know

On the first warm afternoon, the bees came out in a slow golden cloud. Margit counted them as they left and counted them again as they returned. The numbers, she said, were a kind of weather report that no newspaper could print.

“They do not hurry, and they do not waste,” she told me. “I have been trying to learn that for sixty years.”
