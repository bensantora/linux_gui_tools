Chapter Three: The High Summer

By July the days were so long that the children had to be coaxed indoors at bedtime, protesting that the sky was still blue. The hay was cut and stacked in great fragrant hills, and the whole valley smelled of warm grass and distant rain.

The ferryman, Tobias, spent his evenings on the dock with a battered harmonica. He played only three songs, but he played them differently each night, depending on the color of the water.

## The Last Light

On the longest day of the year, the village gathered on the hill to watch the sun go down. Nobody spoke. When the final sliver of gold slipped behind the ridge, someone lit a single lantern, and then another, until the hillside was dotted with small flames.

It was, I think, the most beautiful thing I have ever seen, and it required nothing but patience and a box of matches.
